## Course Structure

This course is broken into topical weekly modules. The weeks start on Monday and end on Sunday. Weeks 1–4 and 6–9 are each broken into four sections: Objectives, Reading, Lecture Notes and Activities.

### Objectives

Each week includes a set of objectives that are helpful to read before starting your work for the week.

### Reading

Links to the reading required for the week will be listed here

### Activities

Activities include lab assignments, code reviews, reading quizzes, and a term project, all of which have due dates. The midterm and final quizzes are closed-book exams taken during weeks 5 and 11.

#### Lab assignments

In weeks 1–4 and 6–9 you will have a lab assignment. This is a hands-on programming assignment.

#### Lab Partners

Each of you will be assigned to a team with one or two lab partners on Canvas. There are different versions of each lab assignment (versions A, B, and C). Each lab partner will do a different version. This allows each student to look at the other’s work and give them help and feedback without seeing a solution to their own assignment.

#### Getting and Giving a Code Review

When you have a beta (draft) version of your lab assignment ready, you will share it in your team's Discord channel. This must be done before the due date for the beta version (by Saturday). Your lab partner will then check your work and complete the "Beta" column of the code review form. You will share the completed review in your Discord team channel and submit it to Canvas before the code review due date (by Tuesday).

#### What to Do with the Code Review You Get

After your lab partner gives you a code review, you may revise your lab work and then fill out the "Production" column of the code review form to indicate your revisions. You will then upload the production version of your lab work to Canvas along with the completed code review form (by Thursday).

#### What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

- Q: What if my lab partner didn’t post a beta version for me to review?

  A: Communicate with them and find out when they will have a beta version ready. Remind them that a beta version doesn’t need to be fully complete. Review their code for them when they send it. As long as you review your partner’s code before the due date for the final version, your review won’t be counted late.

- Q: What if I have already communicated with my lab partner and they haven’t responded or they have said they won’t be posting a beta version anytime soon?

  A: You can do a code review for someone else. Or, you can post an offer in the Discord discussion channel to see if anyone wants an additional review of their most recent lab.

- Q: What if my lab partner didn’t send me a code review by the deadline?

  A: You can ask someone else to give you a review, or as long as you have already posted your beta version, you can review your own code and put a note in the comments section saying your partner didn't give you a review by the deadline.

#### Additional Questions

- Q: Can I do a code review for someone else in addition to my lab partner? 

  A: Sure, it is good experience for you and helpful for the other person.  They just need to have done a different assignment than the one you  did. 

- Q: Can I get more than one code review for a particular lab? 

  A:  Sure, it’s fine for someone else, in addition to your lab partner, to  review your code. As long as they didn’t do the same assignment that you did.
